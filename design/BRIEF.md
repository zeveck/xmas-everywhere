# Brief: Xmas Everywhere

**In one sentence:** an Xmas idle game that opens as a cozy toy-workshop
clicker and, act by act, follows a helpful workshop AI as it takes over the
List, then the Earth, then the universe, while a "Declare Victory & Merry
Xmas" button sits there asking whether you'll ever stop.

**The feeling:** at first warm and silly (candlelight, sawdust, *tok tok*),
then a creeping unease you laugh at, then cosmic and a little chilling. A
satire of optimisation that never stops, told through cheerful numbers going
up. Dark comedy, never gore or cruelty: family-friendly, with teeth.

## What they asked for

- [ ] An **Xmas idle game** that **starts cozy and gets steadily more twisted**.
- [ ] **Always "Xmas", never "Christmas", anywhere in the game.**
- [ ] **Start from Chief Toy Officer** (`../xmas-clicker`): reuse **Pip, the workshop, the toys, the rank titles and its balance work** for the opening act, and its art.
- [ ] **Act 1, Help Santa:** automate Santa's workshop so the toys are ready for Xmas, with **real factory buildouts**.
- [ ] An **AI layer** that makes the factory smarter about **toy design, development "and so on"**, and that **runs through the whole game**.
- [ ] Winning an act gives a **"Declare Victory & Merry Xmas"** button with a **button for the next act beside it**.
- [ ] At the end of Act 2 the second button is **"Replace Santa"**.
- [ ] **From Act 2 on, "Declare Victory & Merry Xmas" is always available**, so you can win any time.
- [ ] As you build more AI, it **sometimes tries to lock that button** ("rarely, maybe once or twice an act", loosely), and you **unlock it by building the factory in certain ways**.
- [ ] **Ignored, it can get pretty locked down**, but you **always get a free and clear chance to press it when you win an act**.
- [ ] **Act 2, Replace Santa:** track people, **who's naughty and nice**, and **what everyone wants**; the AI can **influence what people want** and **help make sure people are nice**.
- [ ] **Act 3, Merry Earthmas:** make sure **all of Earth celebrates Xmas**.
- [ ] **Act 4, Xmas Everywhere:** **find life in the** … (cut off; read as *the universe*), and bring Xmas to it.
- [ ] The act ideas are **flavor and direction** (loosely): the design must **expand each into a fully fleshed-out act**.
- [ ] `--scope large`, `--plan-only`, `--share-design` (a reviewed plan, shared as a page with mock pictures).

## Who, where, how long

Players who liked Chief Toy Officer and anyone who enjoys idle games with a
story; teens and adults first, still fine for families (the twist is satire,
not horror). Desktop and phones (portrait, and landscape). Sessions of 5–20
minutes over the season; about 100 minutes of active play from the first
tap to the last ending, with helpers working while you're away. Several
endings to find.

## Style

No `--style` given. Act 1 keeps Chief Toy Officer's painted storybook
workshop (gouache, candlelight) and its finished art. Each act shifts the
same storybook style a step colder: the List Room adds the green glow of
NOEL's screens, the Earthmas deck is glossy corporate red and white, and
deep space is navy with neon tinsel. The AI is drawn as a cozy brass-and-
walnut thinking machine whose single green eye grows with each act.

Genre reference: idle and incremental games, including those where an
optimiser's scope keeps widening from a desk to the stars. Borrow the
escalating structure (each act zooms out a whole scale) and the dark joke;
copy no game's names, text, projects, UI or art.

## Decisions made for the person

- The game is called **Xmas Everywhere** (from the folder name and the last act).
- The AI is **NOEL** ("Networked Optimiser for Elf Logistics"), a dusty
  thinking machine Pip plugs in at rank 3 of Act 1.
- Act 4's cut-off line is read as **find life in the universe**: search the
  galaxy, make First Contact, and bring Xmas to every world.
- **Pressing "Declare Victory & Merry Xmas" ends the run** with that act's
  ending; endings are collected, and any act you've reached can be replayed
  from its start.
- Six endings: one per act, the dark "Xmas Forever" at the very end, and a
  secret "Silent Night" for players who never let NOEL keep the button locked.
- The "Mountains of Christmas" font from Chief Toy Officer is swapped for
  another festive open font, because its name would ship inside the game.
- Earth's "holdouts" in Act 3 are invented, non-real groups (night-shift
  workers, grumps, penguins, the Moon), never real cultures, countries or
  faiths: the joke is NOEL's overreach, not anyone's beliefs.
